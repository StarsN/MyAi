Recently, many people have asked me why I am optimistic about Q. I find it a bit tiring to answer each individual query, so here I will briefly describe my personal journey.

What attracts me the most are the founder, Cassie, the community atmosphere, and some wise early supporters.

I came across Q around early March this year. Like many others, I initially had no idea what this project was. At that time, Peter had just set up the Discord server. I joined and asked how to run a Q node. As a miner, I was hoping to mine more at a lower cost and then sell to the buyer.

The plan was to launch Q 1.5 on March 15th, but since Q is a highly innovative project, there were many issues during development and delivery, causing multiple delays. Cassie, committed to stable delivery, was constantly fixing bugs. Even though there were many delays, the community members, believing in the project and recognizing her hard work, did not blame her. Instead, they used to joke and console each other on Discord with messages like:
Every day new delay, we never get token, we now get no wallet, we have to wait again, 1.5 never come, airdrop never come, I can not afford node much longer, I can not afford NFT, I can not wait longer, no more delay, my node has 0 peer for weeks, I have no token, no balance, nothing, nobody talks about this on https://t.co/XnKQlOb5iv, nobody cares for us.

At that moment, I suddenly felt that the Q community was full of life, different from other communities. This was a feeling I hadn't experienced for about two years. I decided that I shouldn’t just be a miner, I needed to dive in and learn more about Q, Cassie, and the community.

Slowly, as I dug deeper, I learned that Cassie was a core developer of Farcaster and previously a senior engineer at Coinbase responsible for MPC. Coincidentally, I am also a developer and had interfaced with Fireblocks, a custody service provider based on MPC. This made me realize how amazing Cassie is.

In the crypto world, most information is obtained from CT, but to my surprise, Q does not have a official Twitter account. Even though after the advent of HTTPS, data transmission is encrypted, data storage is still on centralized servers. A developer can access and tamper with your data if they want. This is the same with Twitter, which Cassie despises. This is why, when you open https://t.co/8f79ddueS9, it says "The Internet is dead." She feels that we live in a fragile world where privacy data is not protected on the internet.

Later, I also joined the Quilibrium Node Runner group, where I saw her solving various issues daily and continuously delivering new features. She listened to feedback from the community, and many people gradually joined in to share some of her development tasks. The community has been growing steadily.

The deeper I understanding this project, the more I grew attached to it. Cassie is an idealist who insists on a fair launch, opposing VCs acquiring $QUIL shares through money (many VCs have been rejected by her). The only way to get $QUIL is by becoming a miner, ensuring fairness for everyone.

When she was at Coinbase, the company wanted to build a chain, which is now known as Base. Initially, Cassie proposed not to base it on the bloated EVM architecture but instead design a new chain more suitable for Coinbase. However, when Coinbase chose to base Q on OP, Cassie decided to leave and fully devote herself to Q. Her commitment to protecting user data was steadfast.

Sometimes, I put myself in her shoes to understand her desire to make the world a better place and create cool products instead of homogeneous garbage.

This is also why Cassie developed Howler a few years ago, a decentralized, non-centralized service-controlled Discord-like app, which is the predecessor of Q. https://t.co/u6OBz7zAhy
Later, I saw her historical casts on https://t.co/nh0LGpIqpY, which showed her visionary thinking. At that moment, I wondered why I hadn't discovered Q earlier and appreciated such a great project sooner.

Q's launch introduced the Release Signer mechanism, making development more streamlined and decentralized. To facilitate more efficient communication, Q introduced Discourse (https://t.co/3yyGigwkOX), which has been operating well for about a month.

From early March to now, roughly four months have passed. My personal experience is that the Q community is growing rapidly with its own rhythm and strong cohesion. Despite encountering many issues, there are always good solutions, and the Q-dev team delivers efficiently and consistently. More and more people are joining the Q-vibes. I hope you can become one of them too.

Actually, members of the Q community feel the same way as I do. I'm just expressing this shared sentiment to help those who are not familiar with Q to better understand it. $QUIL $wQUIL #LLTI
