Overview for Q 2.0 Supply and Demand

Emission of Q
A few months ago, Q's emission was approximately 3.3M per day. With the increase in network storage, it has followed the emission formula below, and one month ago, the emission rate dropped to less than 2M per day:
Network storage size (d) in GB (ceiling) is rolled into the highest level of proofs and follows a simple curve, but the curve is generationally (n) bounded and decays over prover group set membership (s, zero-indexed) – (1/((d/1048576)^(1/2^n)))/2^(s).

With the recent addition of new nodes and increased cross-mint requests, network storage has further increased, hence the emission will further decrease. It is estimated that after 2.0 goes live, the daily emission will drop below 1M, possibly even lower.

The price is influenced by supply and demand; discussing the price without considering supply and demand is nonsense.

Supply Post 2.0 Launch
After 2.0 goes live, approximately 150 million $QUIL will be newly claimable. However, the actual selling pressure might not be significant:
- Early supporters who have already profited and continue mining Q will likely not add to the selling pressure post-2.0 since they won't have Q in hand to sell.
- Long-term supporters who haven't claimed Q to sell so far are even less likely to do so after 2.0, hence, no selling pressure from them as well.
- Many small miners entered the space after version 1.4.18, where production was based on the mini-PoMW model with relatively low yields. Most of these miners sell directly, making them a primary source of supply.

Assuming 20% will form the supply, around 30M Q will create selling pressure. If we estimate the price to be $0.30, about 9M USDT is needed to absorb this selling pressure.
